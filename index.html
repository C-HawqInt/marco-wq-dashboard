How to read this

Fish and marine life need dissolved oxygen (DO) to breathe, measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L). Water above 5 mg/L is healthy; 2 to 5 puts fish under stress; below 2 is dangerous. Oxygen is usually lowest near the canal floor, so C-HAWQ profiles the full water column on every cast.

Water quality 101

What is dissolved oxygen?

Dissolved oxygen (DO) is the oxygen gas mixed into the water itself — the air that fish, crabs, and shrimp pull across their gills to breathe. It is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L). When it runs low, animals that can't leave are stressed or suffocate, and a sharp drop is what drives a fish kill.

Two things set it. Sunlight lets seagrass and algae make oxygen during the day, so levels climb by afternoon and fall overnight. And warm, salty, still water near the canal floor holds less oxygen and mixes slowly — so the bottom is almost always the first place to run short. Healthy oxygen is a sign a canal trades water well with the bay.

About the data. C-HAWQ collected these casts {{DATE_FIRST}} through {{DATE_LAST}} with a HOBO depth-profiling sonde lowered from each dock. Depth-chart values are 0.5-ft depth-binned averages across the cast; temperature is shown in °F and depths in feet; dissolved oxygen is salinity-adjusted. Each site's verdict follows the oxygen measured nearest the canal floor.

Why C-HAWQ tests oxygen. The City of Marco Island's monitoring tracks nutrients, pH, and salinity, but not dissolved oxygen. C-HAWQ's dock testing is currently the only source of dissolved-oxygen data for these canals.

Health thresholds. Fish dissolved-oxygen guide — below 2 mg/L lethal, 2 to 5 stressed, 5 or more healthy (UF/IFAS Extension FA-27). Marine saturation floor of 42% (Florida DEP Rule 62-302.533, F.A.C.). Clarity, salinity, and temperature are C-HAWQ field observations, not regulatory limits.
